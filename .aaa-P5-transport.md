## What the worklist said, and what the source said back

Two of the three items in the P5 brief do not survive contact with the code.
Reporting that first, because the re-judge needs to know which numbers are
*already* right before it reads the frames.

### Item 1 — "no planetshine on the ship" — **half wrong, half real**

The brief points at `Game.js:399`, `bounceLight` at intensity 0 commented "used
only when landed", and concludes there is no planetshine in orbit.

There is. It is `shineLight`, built twenty lines above at :375, driven by a
dedicated loop in `_updateWorld` (was :2515-2579), and it is one of the better
reasoned blocks in the file: solid angle × lit fraction of the visible face,
albedo × star colour as a *product* rather than a lerp (with a comment recording
that the lerp version was tried and washed a blue world white), and the same
world handed to the hull a second time as an analytic *disc of radiance*
(`uShineK`/`uShineCol`) so polished trim has something to mirror. `bounceLight`
is the landed ground bounce and is a different light doing a different job. The
two were conflated.

Measured against the review set's own poses, at the reference orbit:

| frame | pose | cover | phase² | shine as % of key |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| h-dry | dist 2.2, phase 58 | 0.207 | 0.585 | **28%** |
| b-terran | dist 1.95, phase 82 | 0.263 | 0.324 | **20%** |
| x-orbit | dist 2.2, phase 134 | 0.207 | 0.023 | **1.1%** |

So planetshine is already a real light on h-dry and b-terran. **x-orbit is a
134° crescent and correctly returns almost nothing — it will not gain
planetshine and should not.** If the re-judge expects x-orbit's belly to wash
ochre, it is expecting the formula to be wrong.

**What is real in item 1 is its second half, and it is worse than stated.**
`fillLight` was constructed at `0x9fc4e0 / 0.55` and the exterior path *only ever
moved it*. Its colour and its level have been boot constants in every space
frame the game has ever rendered. Worse, `updateSurfaceLight` writes both of
them every landed frame and nothing puts them back on liftoff, so after one
landing the space fill is whatever the last frame on the ground left behind.
That is the actual "a ship over an ochre world carries a fixed blue fill it has
no source for" defect, and it is fixed below.

### Item 2 — "the practical is captured mid-ramp" — **falsified by arithmetic**

`updateGlowLight` lerps at `dt*3`, i.e. a 1/3 s time constant. `judgeset.mjs`
passes `settle: 2200` for l-resonator, `probe.mjs` has no pause and the rAF loop
runs throughout — that is **6.6 time constants, 99.86% converged**. The light is
not caught near zero.

The magnitude claim does not hold either, and I nearly wrote the wrong fix
before checking. `buildTine` **publishes** a practical (`Structures.js:1033`,
irradiance 1.25 at radius 12), so `readPractical` wins and the `intensity: 1.4`
fallback in `glowSources()` is dead code. Through three's windowed inverse
square with `reach = 5*radius = 60`, at the shot's 10.2-unit standoff the
published spec delivers **1.73 against a key of 3.4 — 51% of the star.** The
resonator's bank is emphatically lighting the ship already.

I reverted my own attempt to raise the fallback and left a comment on it
explaining that it is unreachable and that its units are not the published
spec's, so the next person does not lose the same hour.

The one thing in item 2 worth keeping is defensive, and it is in.

### Item 3 — "set-piece fill" — **real, but small by construction**

Implemented. Be aware of what the geometry allows: a station or derelict at the
distances the review set poses returns very little (m-derelict at 3.2 radii with
a phase of 96° works out to ~1% of key — a derelict really is a poor reflector
seen small). It only becomes a light inside about two radii, which is docking
range. **Expect this to change the approach in play, not the judged stills.**

## What changed

### 1. The exterior fill is a share of a budget, not a boot constant

New block in `_updateWorld`, immediately after the planetshine loop (it needs
that loop's result).

```
shineShare = clamp(shineLight.intensity / (sunIntensity * 0.30), 0, 1)
fillLight.color     = VOID_FILL lerped 0.80*shineShare toward the shine hue,
                      the hue first scaled to VOID_FILL_LUMA
fillLight.intensity = 0.524 * min(1.6, 0.55 + illum*0.5) * (1 - 0.45*shineShare)
```

- **Hue.** `shineLight.color` is luminance-normalised to 1.0, so mixing toward
  it raw would raise the fill's *level* by a third on the way past. It is scaled
  to the void tint's own luminance (0.746) first, so changing the fill's colour
  cannot change its brightness. On h-dry the fill goes 76% of the way to the
  desert's ochre; on x-orbit's crescent it stays cobalt, which is correct.
- **Level.** The `min(1.6, 0.55 + illum*0.5)` is `rimLight`'s expression, term
  for term, taken deliberately: the rim and the fill stand in for the same
  thing (starlight arriving from anywhere but straight down the key) and have no
  business disagreeing about how it varies with the star. Only the leading
  coefficient is new and it is *solved*, not picked: `0.524 * 1.05 = 0.550` at
  `illum = 1`, which is exactly the value the game has been shipping. **A frame
  with nothing near it is unchanged by construction.** Far out (`illum` floored
  at 0.30) the fill now falls to 0.367 instead of holding 0.55 against a key
  that has dropped to 1.02 — the same inversion the landed rig's key/fill
  comment documents at length.
- **The 0.45 gate.** At full share the invented fill is cut by 45%. Total
  shadow-side illumination falls by about a seventh, not the full stop the
  landed comment records from cutting a fill on its own — the point is that the
  light is *redistributed* into a real source, not removed.

### 2. `bounceLight` in space: set-piece fill, and a staleness bug closed

`_stageSpace` re-adds `bounceLight` to the exterior scene (so the light count
does not change across the swap and no material recompiles) but nothing in the
exterior path ever wrote to it. **Land, lift off, and the ship keeps a
directional aimed straight up out of a planet that is no longer there, at
whatever level the last frame of the landing set, for the rest of the session.**
That is a real light in every space frame after the first landing, from below.

Rather than clamp it to zero, it now does out here what it does down there: it
is the nearest large surface that is not the sky. Planetshine's own maths
applied to stations and anomalies — solid angle × lit fraction of the visible
face — with a 0.30 fill factor (a truss station is mostly holes; judged as a
solid Lambertian sphere it would return about three times too much) and a cap at
22% of key. Tint is the star's own, because bare structure and painted metal are
grey and there is no excuse for inventing a hue. Decays to zero when nothing
qualifies, which is also the bug fix.

There is a guard against normalising a zero vector when the floating origin
coincides with the set-piece: a NaN light direction blanks every material in the
scene and is not a defect you can look at and diagnose.

### 3. The roaming practical follows the frame, not the hull

`updateGlowLight` selected the nearest source **to `ship.absPos`**. In every mode
the player has, that is the camera to within one hull length. It is *not* the
camera in `inspect()`, which parks the lens on a sphere around a chosen subject
and leaves the ship where the previous shot left it — which is exactly how
**v-station** is staged (`m-derelict` poses the ship at the derelict, then
`v-station` flies only the camera to the station). A station framed from 2.4 of
its own radii with the hull parked at a derelict had its own bay floods
**switched off**, because the only body inside the reach test was one the camera
was nowhere near. Selection now takes the nearer of ship and `camAbs`: one extra
distance per source, no behavioural change in play, and the practical follows the
picture.

*Caveat for the re-judge:* this is a no-op if the derelict and the station
happen to be close together in the seeded system. If v-station's bay floods
were already on, this changed nothing there.

### 4. Snap the practical on only when there is nothing to snap from

`if (L.intensity < 1e-3) L.intensity = want; else <ramp as before>`. The 1/3 s
ramp exists so flying between two lit set-pieces does not pop, and it stays for
that. Arriving out of the dark — a fold, a jump, a posed camera — there is no
previous value to cross-fade with and the ramp is just a third of a second of
being wrong. Gating on "the lamp is effectively off" keeps the anti-pop property
by construction. Robustness against short settles, **not** the l-resonator fix;
per the arithmetic above there was nothing to fix there.

## Cost

**Zero measurable frame cost, no new lights, no new passes, no new draw calls.**

- One extra pass over `this.bodies` (tens of entries) doing a `distanceTo` and
  an early `continue`; the normalise/dot pair runs only for bodies subtending
  more than ~5.7°, i.e. approximately never.
- One extra `distanceTo` per glow source (a handful of anomalies and two
  stations).
- Four scalar ops and two `Color` writes for the fill budget.
- Two module-level constants and one scratch `Color` allocated at load.

The light *count* seen by every material is unchanged, which is the number that
actually costs anything in a forward renderer.

## What I deliberately did not do

- **Did not raise `HULL_LIGHT.uBounce`'s multiplier** (end of `_updateWorld`:
  `shineLight.color * (0.012 + I*0.030) + 0.010`). This is the term most
  directly responsible for greeble-kit shadow sides sitting at 0-8/255, and it
  is one line in my file. But wl.md §4.3 states that P2's nebula raise (3-4×)
  moves the PMREM the same materials fill from, and P1 moves the black point
  everything is measured through. Raising `uBounce` now would stack three
  independent lifts blindly on the same pixels. **Flagging it as the obvious
  next single lever once P1 and P2 have landed.**
- **Did not touch the planetshine formula, its coefficients, or its direction
  logic.** Every one of them carries a comment recording a measurement.
  Likewise the rim's 0.62, the anti-key geometry, and the `uShineK`/`uShineCol`
  analytic disc.
- **Did not put stations into the planetshine loop.** That would feed the
  analytic disc (`uShineK`) too, and the comment there is explicit that the disc
  is a *smooth body of radiance* for the mirror term. A station is not one.
- **Did not raise the published Tine or station practical intensities.** Both
  carry calibration comments against the key at a stated standoff, and both
  measure correctly when I redo the arithmetic.
- **Did not touch `fitGroundShadow`** — wl.md §4.5 names the cascade split as a
  later piece that collides with P4.
- **Did not make the planetshine loop camera-aware** the way the practical now
  is. The practical is a point light with a position, so following the camera is
  a one-line change to a distance test. Shine's *direction*, `uShineV` and the
  analytic disc are all built around the ship and the floating origin, so
  camera-anchoring it is a rework rather than an edit — and the one frame where
  it would show, w-traffic, is a shot-setup failure that belongs to P10.
- **Note on the set-piece fill:** since it is a directional aimed from the
  set-piece toward the origin, it also lands weakly on the set-piece's own
  ship-facing side. Capped at 22% of key and only reaching that inside two
  radii, this is well under the noise; left alone rather than special-cased.

## What the re-judge should look at specifically

1. **h-dry and b-terran, ship shadow side.** These are where the fill hue change
   has the most authority (shine share 0.95 and 0.67). Sample the ship's shadow
   side in h-dry and in e-barren: they measured the same flat cool blue-grey
   before and must now differ, with h-dry warmer. This is the headline claim.
2. **x-orbit is expected to be nearly unchanged**, and that is the correct
   result for a 134° crescent, not a failed fix. Do not tune against it.
3. **v-station.** Check whether the bay floods are now doing work on the near
   structure. If they still are not, log `g.glowLight.intensity` and
   `g.glowLight.position` at capture — the remaining candidate is the station
   being outside `reach = 5 * 0.62 = 3.1` units of *both* ship and camera.
4. **l-resonator.** The practical is delivering ~51% of key. If the hull still
   carries no green after P1, the cause is the output stage or the hull's own
   material response, **not** this light. Log `g.glowLight.intensity` to settle
   it — the expected value is `1.25 * 12² = 180` candela.
5. **Every space frame far from the star** (outer-system moons, ice worlds): the
   fill is now 33% lower there than it was. Combined with P1's black point this
   is the one place my change could over-darken. If it does, the lever is the
   0.524, and the number to preserve is 0.550 at `illum = 1`.
6. **Regression to check:** land, lift off, and shoot a space frame. The ship
   should no longer be lit from below by the ground it left.
