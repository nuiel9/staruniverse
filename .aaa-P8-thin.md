# P8 — Thin geometry and specular aliasing

**File touched:** `src/gfx/greeble.js` — one hunk, +49/-1, inside `RIM_HOOK`.
Nothing else. `src/world/Station.js` was read but not edited.

Two of the package's three items did not survive contact with the code. The
third turned out to have a different cause than the worklist proposed, and that
is what I fixed.

---

## Item 1 — "extend MATTE_HOOK with geometric specular AA". **Already shipped. Not done.**

The worklist asks for

```
vec3 dnx=dFdx(vShipNrm), dny=dFdy(vShipNrm);
float var=0.5*(dot(dnx,dnx)+dot(dny,dny));
roughness = sqrt(clamp(roughness*roughness + min(2.0*var,0.18), 0.0, 1.0));
```

That mechanism is in the file already, at `greeble.js:1404-1411`, installed on
`#include <normal_fragment_maps>`:

```
vec3 dnx = dFdx(normal), dny = dFdy(normal);
float nvar = dot(dnx, dnx) + dot(dny, dny);
float widen = min(nvar*0.68, 0.24);
roughnessFactor = sqrt(clamp(roughnessFactor*roughnessFactor + widen, 0.0, 1.0));
```

It is the same formula and it is placed **better** than the proposal in two
ways. It takes the variance of the *final shaded* normal, which is the geometric
normal plus the hull's own relief — a superset of `vShipNrm`, and the relief is
the larger contributor at close range. And `<normal_fragment_maps>` runs after
`<roughnessmap_fragment>` and before `<lights_physical_fragment>`, so the widen
lands on top of the plating law's own roughness work rather than underneath it;
hooking `<lights_physical_fragment>` as proposed would have had to re-derive it.

Beyond that, three r185's own `lights_physical_fragment` (verified in
`node_modules/three/src/renderers/shaders/ShaderChunk/lights_physical_fragment.glsl.js:7-11`)
adds `geometryRoughness` from `dFdx/dFdy(nonPerturbedNormal)` to
`material.roughness` for **every** physical material in the scene. So dressed
hulls already carry two independent layers of geometric specular AA and even the
undressed `skyLit` glass carries one.

I did **not** retune `0.68 / 0.24` toward the worklist's `1.0 / 0.18`. The
comment above the block defends the cap by name ("past a quarter this stops
being anti-aliasing and starts sanding the trim rings flat wherever the geometry
is dense"), the existing pair is more aggressive than the proposal exactly where
aliasing is worst, and I have no way to measure the trade from here. Changing it
would be different, not better.

## Item 2 — "vertex-stage minimum projected width for strut/rail instances". **Not done, and I believe it cannot be done well here.**

Four separate reasons, in order of how badly each one breaks it:

1. **The alpha half has nowhere to go.** The trick only works because the widened
   member is dimmed by `trueWidth/minWidth`; widening without dimming just makes
   a distant rail a *fat* line, which is worse than a thin one. That fade needs
   either alpha blending — impossible, these are opaque merged meshes sharing one
   material with the whole station, and `transparent:true` would put the station
   into the sorted pass and wreck its depth order — or alpha-to-coverage, which
   needs MSAA on the scene render target, which this project's own triage
   explicitly discarded (worklist §67).
2. **They are not instances.** Struts, trusses and rails are merged into the same
   opaque geometry as everything else in the asset; there is no per-part data at
   all. Widening a cylinder in screen space needs its cross-section axis and its
   true radius per vertex, so the fix requires a new vertex attribute authored in
   `truss()`/`strut()`/`solarWing()`.
3. **That attribute cannot be added safely from inside my file.** `Station.js`,
   `Structures.js` and `Fleet.js` all push geometry from their own local helpers
   (`bar()`, `latheZ()`, `loft()`) into the same weld lists that receive
   `truss()` output — `Station.js:1457-1459` and `:1649-1659` mix them directly.
   `mergeGeometries` requires identical attribute sets, so a channel present on
   some members of a list and absent on others returns null and the station
   disappears. It could be normalised inside `weld()`, but `hull.js` and
   `Structures.js` also call `mergeGeometries` directly, which is exactly the
   class of cross-file coupling I was told to stop at rather than reach for.
4. **It needs a pixel-scale uniform nobody updates.** Projected width in pixels
   needs the drawing-buffer height; there is no built-in for it, and the only
   in-file way to feed it would be an `onBeforeRender` hook installed per welded
   mesh. More machinery than the item is worth.

The file already answers this defect at build time, and says so at
`greeble.js:2068-2082`: `GAUGE = 1/300`, "a member thinner than about a pixel
does not render as a thin member ... the fix is gauge". That comment is a
measured decision about the same failure and, per the house rule, it wins.

One variant I considered and rejected: darken toward black instead of fading
alpha — against a near-black sky that approximates coverage well. It needs the
identical attribute and uniform machinery, so it fails on reasons 2-4 anyway.

## Item 3 — "edge-on docking vanes blown to clipped white polygons". **Fixed, with a different cause than the worklist assumed.**

This is not specular aliasing. It is the shared rim term flooding a whole face.

In `RIM_HOOK` the backlit limb light is

```
fres  = pow(1 - dot(normal, Vv), 3.2)
back  = clamp(-dot(uSunV, Vv))
graze = clamp(1 - abs(dot(normal, uSunV)))
directSpecular += uSunTint * fres * back * graze * uRimGain * 2.6
```

Once the key is behind the subject, `uSunV ≈ -Vv`, so "turned away from the
camera" and "turned away from the star" are the *same* condition: `fres` and
`graze` are no longer two independent gates, they saturate together. On a body
with a radius that is correct — all three reach one inside the last couple of
per cent of the limb and you get the thin bright edge the term is for.

On a **flat plate seen edge-on** they reach one across the entire face at once.
`Game.js:2644` sets `uSunTint = sunColor * min(2.2, 0.35 + illum)`, so the worst
case is `2.2 * 2.6 = 5.7` of additive specular on every pixel of the blade. AgX
cannot recover 5.7; it clips to paper white and the blade loses its silhouette.

The vanes named in the finding are `Station.js:1688-1696` — `panel([[0,9.4],
[7.0,9.7], [8.6,12.0], [0,12.4]], 1.2, 0.30)`, four flat blades standing
radially off the docking collar so the camera sees them edge-on over most of an
approach. Exactly the failure case.

**The change:** gate the rim gain on curvature — how far the interpolated
geometric normal (`nonPerturbedNormal`, three's own pre-relief normal) swings per
pixel.

```
vec3 gnx = dFdx(nonPerturbedNormal), gny = dFdy(nonPerturbedNormal);
float curve = sqrt(dot(gnx, gnx) + dot(gny, gny));
float limb = mix(0.30, 1.0, smoothstep(0.0005, 0.0030, curve));
```

Physically: light wraps a curved limb because there is progressively less matter
in its way as the surface turns. Around the flat face of an opaque slab there is
no wrap at all — whatever glow the blade is owed belongs to its 300 mm bevelled
edge, and the bevel still collects it, because the bevel is where the normal
turns.

**What the numbers were tuned against.** With `fres` at power 3.2, the rim's own
half-strength point on a barrel of projected radius R pixels sits at 0.98R, where
the normal turns about 5.2/R radians per pixel: 0.10 on a fifty-pixel nacelle,
0.026 on a two-hundred-pixel one, 0.010 on a five-hundred-pixel hull filling the
frame, 0.0052 on a thousand-pixel station drum.

The ramp **top** is the load-bearing constant, and it has to clear the *largest*
radius in the game rather than the smallest, because the big masses are the ones
whose limbs turn slowest. My first pass had it at 0.0080, which only holds full
strength out to R ≈ 650 px — u-shipclose and the v-station drum project past
that, and the rim's inner tail would have dimmed on exactly the curved masses
this change is supposed to leave alone. Corrected to 0.0030, which is full
strength out to R ≈ 1700 px.

It can be set that low without risk because the discrimination needs no margin:
a planar face returns `curve` of exactly 0.0, bit for bit, since
`normalize(vNormal)` is constant across it. Anything with any radius at all sits
above the ramp; only flat plate sits under it.

The floor is 0.30 and not zero because this term's first job is the one stated at
the top of the file: a backlit hull must not be a hole in the starfield. A flat
backlit face keeps 1.7, which is bright and is not clipped.

`nonPerturbedNormal` rather than `normal` matters: the derivative of the shaded
normal would read every plate seam and weld bead as curvature and gate nothing.
Verified in scope and unconditional for r185
(`normal_fragment_begin.glsl.js:74`), for both `MeshStandardMaterial` (`dress`)
and `MeshPhysicalMaterial` (`skyLit`, the glass). No material in this repo sets
`flatShading`, which would have zeroed the derivative and floored everything.

### Side effect I consider a win, not a risk

`truss()` builds longerons at 5-6 segments. Those step 60-72° per facet, well
over `creaseNormals`' 32° threshold, so they stay hard-faceted and their facets
are planar — `curve` is zero on them and they drop to the 0.30 floor. A backlit
truss member 1-2 px wide currently gets 5.7 across most of its width, which is a
solid white line, and that is a real part of the "handrails/truss as candy-cane
dotted lines" finding arriving through the specular channel rather than through
coverage. Handrails are 14-sided (26° steps, creased smooth per the comment at
`greeble.js:1869`), sweep their normal fast, and keep the full rim — which is the
correct limb read for a genuinely round thin member.

## Cost

About ten ALU per fragment on hull materials, and probably fewer: three's
`lights_physical_fragment` already evaluates `dFdx(nonPerturbedNormal)` and
`dFdy(nonPerturbedNormal)` a few lines earlier for its `geometryRoughness` term,
so both are live and a competent compiler common-subexpression-eliminates mine.
No new uniforms, attributes, varyings, textures, draw calls or passes; no CPU
work; no change to geometry, memory or shader permutation count. This cannot
move the frame time measurably.

## Not verified visually

Per the brief I did not run the dev server or take shots, so **the shader was
never compiled** — `node --check src/gfx/greeble.js` validates the JavaScript
around the GLSL, not the GLSL itself, which only fails at runtime link. What
static checking can and did establish:

- Backtick count in the file is unchanged at 69, so nothing was introduced into
  the GLSL template literals.
- The one new GLSL identifier, `nonPerturbedNormal`, is declared unconditionally
  and at the same scope in the actual r185 chunk source
  (`normal_fragment_begin.glsl.js:74`), and `<normal_fragment_begin>` precedes
  `<lights_fragment_end>` in `meshphysical.glsl.js`.
- The inserted text contains neither `CAVITY` nor `PLUME_LIGHT`, and both
  `.replace()` targets that consume `RIM_HOOK` sit textually above the insertion
  point, so the first-occurrence substitutions in `skyLit()` and `dress()` are
  unaffected.

## What the re-judge should look at specifically

- **v-station, the docking collars.** The four guide vanes should read as dark
  blades with a bright bevelled edge instead of white slabs. This is the one
  frame that proves or disproves the change.
- **Any backlit silhouette — k-ship, u-shipclose, a-spawn.** Confirm the limb
  light on curved masses (nacelles, tanks, the barrel) is *unchanged*. If it
  dimmed, the knob is the ramp's **upper** edge, 0.0030, not the lower one: a
  large mass turns its normal slowly, so it fails the *top* of the ramp first.
  Halving it to 0.0015 doubles the projected radius that keeps full strength.
- **A camera orbit around v-station.** Trusses should sit dimmer and steadier.
  They will not stop being thin — that is item 2, which is still open and which I
  do not think can be closed without either MSAA on the scene target or the
  per-vertex gauge channel described above.
- **Whether any flat panel now reads too dark when backlit.** The 0.30 floor is
  the only number here I could not derive from the term's own falloff; it is a
  judgement call and it is the one to move if the frames say so.
