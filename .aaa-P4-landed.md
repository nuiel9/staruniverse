# P4 — Landed lighting and air

**File touched: `src/world/Surface.js` only.** No other file was read for edit, none
was written. Nothing outside the package's list was needed.

`node --check src/world/Surface.js` passes. No backticks were introduced inside any
GLSL template literal (the two backticks I did add are in plain-JS block comments in
`update()`, which is house style — see `Game.js:1771`).

All four items landed. Two of them landed differently from the way the worklist
worded them, because the worklist's literal instruction does not survive contact with
the arithmetic. Both deviations are spelled out below.

---

## 1. Vegetation self-illuminated at dusk — **closed**

`FLORA_FRAG:5305` and `TREE_FRAG:5486` add an inter-plant bounce tinted with `uCVeg`.
`uCVeg` is a palette entry, so the term had the same magnitude at midnight as at
midday. Worked through on the terran world the dusk frame is shot on, at the sun
elevation `judgeset.mjs` searches for (it breaks when `uSunDir.y` lands in
`0.03 … 0.10`), what a horizontal leaf receives from the star — key, reddened by that
air mass, then foreshortened — is about **1/30** of its zenith value, while this term
had not moved at all. It was therefore the dominant light on every plant in the
frame, and it was washing the ground's per-channel solar extinction back out. That is
the mechanism behind both "vegetation self-illuminated at dusk" and "grass illuminant
doesn't track the sky".

New uniform `uVegLit` (`Surface.js:6528`, written at `:7469`), multiplied into both
terms. It is the illuminant a horizontal leaf actually receives —
`mean(uSunColor)·max(sunY,0) + mean(uSkyColor)` — over the same quantity at a
reference elevation, clamped to 1.

**Deviation:** the worklist says "normalised so the noon look is unchanged by
construction". Taken literally that means normalising at the zenith — but a landing
site almost never sees its star overhead, so a zenith reference quietly dims foliage
in *every* daylight frame and the "y-landed pixel-identical" verify fails. The
reference is **a thirty-degree sun (`REF_Y = 0.5`)**, computed from the same
expressions with `day` and `nf` at their daylight values. Above 30° the ratio clamps
to 1 and daylight foliage is bit-identical.

Paper numbers on the terran world, `betaR = (1.94, 4.47, 10.29)`, ground
`betaM = 7.47 × 0.21`:

| sun.y | leaf illuminant | `uVegLit` |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00 (zenith) | 2.78 | 1.00 |
| 0.50 (reference) | 1.42 | 1.00 |
| 0.35 (20°) | 1.02 | 0.72 |
| 0.08 (judged dusk) | 0.363 | **0.256** — two stops down |

**Open factual risk the re-judge should check:** `y-landed` is shot at `landed.t = 0`
with no sun manipulation, so I could not determine its sun elevation without running
the app. If it is above 30° the foliage is bit-identical. If it sits between about 22°
and 30°, foliage bounce dims by up to ~17% — `imgdiff` will flag it and it should be
read as the intended behaviour arriving slightly early, not as a regression. If it is
below 21°, `y-landed` also picks up a trace of item 2's warm fill.

## 2. Shadow fill blue under an orange sky — **closed, mechanism corrected**

`uSkyColor` is built from *normalised* Rayleigh (hue fixed for the life of the world)
plus a deliberately blue-biased floor. It is the same cobalt at a two-degree sun as at
noon.

**Deviation — the worklist's fix would have made it worse.** It says to blend toward
`uInsM` by `(1 - day)`. On this world at the judged dusk sun, `uInsM` is about
**1/30 of the fill in luminance**, so a 0.5 blend toward it does not warm the shadows,
it removes half of them. What is right about the proposal is the *hue*: `sc·att` is
the star after the air has had it. So the target is `sc·att` **renormalised to the
fill's own luminance**, and only its hue is used. `lumSky` downstream is therefore
exactly unchanged, which matters because `uDecalK` is computed from it.

The gain is derived, not picked. Fill at the judged sun is in the ratio
`0.137 : 0.250 : 0.482`; `sc·att` at that air mass is `0.834 : 0.494 : 0.156`. Scale
the second to the first's luminance and blend by `kw`:

- `kw < 0.43` → still blue-dominant, no visible change
- `kw ≈ 0.43` → **green becomes the minimum channel** (warm-violet begins)
- `kw ≈ 0.53` → red passes blue
- `kw > 0.60` → plain orange, green back in the middle — too far

The curve `min(0.58, (1−day)·clamp(sunY·14+0.85)·thick·1.32)` puts the judged frame at
**0.51**, mid-band. `day` saturates at a 21° sun, so every daylight frame gets exactly
zero. The elevation gate carries it to about −4° and then lets go, because civil
twilight is warm and midnight is not. Gated on `hasAir` and scaled by `thick`, matching
the existing horizon-warm term at `:7502`.

Expected: rocks, cliffs and trunks in shadow go warm-violet instead of cobalt, and
the whole dusk frame stops having two colour temperatures that disagree.

## 3. No aerial-perspective gradient in the mid-distance — **closed**

`uDustH` **75 → 250 m** for `hasAir`; `uExtD` **air/3600 → air/2000** for `hasAir`.

The scale height is the structural half: at 75 m the boundary layer is spent before it
reaches anything with a silhouette, so a hill 200 m above the basin floor stands
entirely clear of it — near ground hazes, hills do not, fog appears to switch on at the
back. 250 m is a real convective boundary layer over warm ground and it puts the
mid-ground hills *inside* the layer.

**Deviation:** the worklist proposed `air/1800`; I used `air/2000`, because raising the
scale height 3.3× already lengthens the column through everything that stands up in it,
and the two compound. At `air/2000` a ridge at 1 km is a little over half veiled and
the first 50 m sits at under 3% — the near field is what `airColumn()`'s closed form
protects and it is where this would milk first.

The airless divisor is left at `air/3600` and `uDustH` at 130 untouched: there is no
boundary layer without air, that column stands in for ground-thrown dust, and it was
measured as it is. The Rayleigh figure at `air/2200` was not touched — its comment
records three reviews.

The stale "the first sixty metres" in `aerial()`'s model comment was amended, with a
pointer to `uDustH` in `update()`.

## 4. Ground shadow decals collapsing at low sun — **closed, and it needed more than
the worklist asked for**

Three changes, and the third is the most prominent deviation in this package.

**(a) `stretch` ceiling.** Was `clamp(sl/max(sunY, 0.20), 0.7, 4.0)` — the two numbers
together meant that from a 14° sun all the way to sunset every stone cast the *same*
stubby oval. Now `clamp(sl/max(sunY, 0.0625), 0.7, 16.0)`; the denominator floor has to
come down or the ceiling is unreachable (1/0.0625 = 16). **16, not the 24 proposed** —
the limit is the mesh, not the physics: the decal is `discFan(6)`, which gives it three
stations along its long axis to follow the ground with. At the judged dusk sun the true
value is ~12.5 and the ceiling never binds, so 24 buys that frame nothing.

**(b) Shape past the old ceiling.** `narrow = 1/(1 + max(stretch−4,0)·0.055)` on the
cross-sun axis — about a third off the width at the judged frame. A raking shadow is a
tongue, and this also keeps the quad's area from growing with the square, which is what
limits how much of it can float off a slope. Identical to before at `stretch ≤ 4`. The
extra `lift` for long shadows is **tapered by `(1 − loc.y)·0.5`** so only the tip rises
(≈0 at the contact end, which is the end whose whole job is to touch).

**(c) The gates — beyond the worklist's instruction, and required.** The worklist says
the elevation gate is "a contributor, not the cause" and names only the stretch clamp.
Stretch alone does not deliver the item's own stated outcome. There were **two**
elevation gates, both reading `uSunDir.y`, both uniform-valued, and they *multiplied*:
`clamp(sunY·7)` in `uDecalK` and `clamp(sunY·6)` in `DECAL_VERT`. At the judged sun
those are 0.35 and 0.30 — a decal arrived at **0.105** of strength before `keyVsFill`,
the term that actually measures how dark a shadow should be, had had its say. That is
double-counting: `keyVsFill` already falls from 0.89 at noon to ~0.55 at dusk on its
own, which is the honest stop-and-a-half.

So: the shader gate is **deleted** (moved into `uDecalK`, where its sibling already
was), and `uDecalK`'s is steepened `clamp(sunY·7)` → `clamp(sunY·20)`, which reaches
full strength by ~3° and is gone at the horizon — which is the only job a gate here has
(no key below the horizon means no cause for a smudge). Neither gate had a comment
defending it.

Net at the judged dusk sun: decal opacity **0.089 → 0.33 (≈3.7×)** and length
**4R → 12.5R (≈3×)**. At noon `uDecalK` is unchanged (both gates were already 1).

## What this costs

Effectively nothing, and no new draw call, pass, texture or light.

- **Fragment:** one scalar multiply on flora and tree fragments (`uVegLit`).
- **Vertex:** on decals only, one `max`, one divide and three multiplies per vertex —
  7 verts × a few thousand near-field instances.
- **CPU, once per frame:** six `Math.exp` and ~40 multiplies for `uVegLit`'s reference,
  plus ~12 for the warm blend. This runs in `Surface.update`, not per object.
- `uDustH` / `uExtD` / `uDecalK` are constants changed in place — identical instruction
  count. Higher dust extinction means more of the mid-distance is in-scatter-dominated,
  which is arithmetic already being done.
- No allocation added: the warm blend writes in place through `U.uSkyColor.value`, the
  reference illuminant is scalars.

## What I deliberately did not do

- **Did not touch the Rayleigh column** (`air/2200`, `:7448`). Its comment records
  three independent reviews and the worklist says leave it. It is right.
- **Did not touch `uInsM`, `uInsD`, `uHazeH`, `KEY`, `lift`, `day`, `nf`, the night
  floor, or `keyVsFill`.** Every one carries a measured justification and none of them
  is what any of these four findings is about.
- **Did not touch the vegetation bounce's hue**, only its independence from the light.
  The argument at `:5296` for why plants must not take the void's fill is sound and is
  left standing verbatim.
- **Did not raise `discFan`'s segment count.** More rim vertices do not add stations
  along the long axis of a fan — the interior is still triangles from one centre — so it
  would cost geometry and fix nothing. The tapered tip lift is the cheap mitigation.
- **Did not touch the shadow cascade** (`fitGroundShadow` / `smask` / `uShadowR`). The
  worklist sequences that third piece after both P4 and P5, and half of it is in
  `Game.js`.

## For the re-judge, specifically

1. **`y-landed` foliage.** `imgdiff` it. Bit-identical if that frame's sun is above 30°;
   up to ~17% dimmer foliage bounce at 22°. Either outcome is intended — see item 1.
1b. **The stated verify for item 1** is "grass R/G must track at least half the sky's
   swing (currently 1/5)". Carried through on paper with a representative sward albedo:
   sky R/G goes 0.478 at noon → 1.14 at the judged dusk (a swing of +0.66, and note the
   dusk end of that is item 2's warm blend); grass R/G goes 0.671 → 1.034 (+0.363).
   Ratio **0.55**, i.e. it passes, but only just — the albedo I assumed is the weak link
   in that estimate, so measure it rather than trusting it.
2. **Warm-violet, not orange.** On `z-landed-dusk`, sample a rock's shadow side. Want R
   and B above G. If G is the *middle* channel, `kw` overshot and the cap at `:7415`
   should come down from 0.58; if the shade is still frankly blue, it undershot.
3. **Near-field milking.** Item 3's risk. Sample the ground 10–50 m from the boots on
   `y-landed` and confirm it has not gone milky. Then saturation at 10 m / 1 km / 4 km
   should now fall monotonically.
4. **Known residual, pre-existing and amplified rather than caused:** `DECAL_VERT`
   rejects on `behindCamera(anchor, …)` — the *caster's* position, not the shadow's. A
   long raking shadow whose stone is just behind the camera pops out at the bottom of
   frame. That was true at 4R and is more visible at 12R. Fixing it properly means
   testing the shadow's own footprint, which is a separate change.
5. **Long decals over broken ground.** Three along-axis stations over 12R of dune. The
   tip lift should keep them out of the hillside; if a shadow visibly spears a rise,
   lower the 16.0 ceiling rather than removing the stretch fix.
6. Everything in this package is judged *through* P1. The dusk aureole, the black floor
   and the CA all sit between these changes and the eye.
