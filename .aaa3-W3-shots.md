# W3 — three judge frames that did not show what they were named for

Files touched: `tools/survey.mjs`, `tools/judgeset.mjs`. Nothing else.
`src/world/Fleet.js` was on my list and I did **not** need it — see (b).

Everything below was captured with Playwright against the running dev server and
looked at. Probes live in the scratch dir, not in the repo.

---

## (a) t-belt was a second copy of x-orbit

**Root cause.** Nothing to do with target selection. The shot's staging was
wrapped in `if (g.fields[0]) { ... }`, and the home system has no belt at all:

```
node tools/probe.mjs "... {fields: g.fields.length, belts: g.system.belts}"
  →  { "fields": 0, "belts": [] }
```

`generateSystem` rolls a belt with p=0.62 and Ithirka lost the roll, so the whole
block was a no-op and the camera stayed exactly where the previous shot (x-orbit)
had left it. The imgdiff the brief quotes — 2.229% of pixels at mad 1.50 — is
star jitter and grain between two captures of the *same camera*.

**Fix.** The shot now stages itself. If the current system has no field it rolls
the whole galaxy in the page (`generateSystem` is pure data — no meshes, no
bakes — so 14 systems cost milliseconds). Measured belt counts across the
galaxy: `0,1,1,2,0,1,1,1,0,1,1,0,1,0` — nine of the fourteen carry one. It
hyperjumps to the first that does, stops the arrival cutscene and stages. It is also
moved to the **end** of `SHOTS`, because it is now the only shot that changes
system on its own initiative and nothing may follow it.

Two consequences handled:

* `PRE` now does `if (g.ship.foldMode) g.toggleFold(false)`. p-fold engages the
  drive and nothing disengaged it; that was harmless while p-fold was last, and
  became load-bearing the moment t-belt moved after it, because `AsteroidField`
  is only updated when `f.contains(ship.absPos) && !ship.foldMode`. The belt
  would have come back empty for a second, unrelated reason.
* The camera swing off the star was re-tuned, because the 1.05 rad in the old
  comment was reasoned about but had never actually been *seen* — the shot it
  was written for never staged a belt. Swept and measured:

  | swing (rad) | mean L | px ≥ 40 | px ≥ 110 |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.30 |  9.6 |  7.7% | 0.8% |
  | 0.75 |  7.3 |  4.0% | 0.9% |
  | 1.05 | 16.6 | 14.1% | 2.5% |
  | 1.40 | 24.6 | 21.4% | 6.7% |
  | **1.80** | **35.0** | **29.5%** | **12.2%** |
  | 2.30 |  7.2 |  5.7% | 0.2% |

  Below ~1.4 the rocks are backlit *and* the auto-exposure is stopping down for
  the star, which is what "dirt on the lens" actually looks like. Past 2.3 the
  star is behind the camera, the rocks go flat and the exposure opens on empty
  sky. 1.8 is the peak; blown pixels stay at 0.04%.

**Measured, before → after** (full survey run, 1600×900):

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| imgdiff t-belt vs x-orbit | 2.229% >10, mad 1.50 | **46.744% >10, mad 37.90** |
| t-belt frame mean | 8.7 | **34.7** |
| t-belt black% | 88.6 | **55.2** |
| asteroids in a frame named for a belt | 0 | a belt, at every depth, plus the tug working it |

x-orbit itself is untouched: `calls=205 tris=980k`, identical to the figures in
the brief.

Both code paths are exercised: run standalone from a cold boot in Ithirka it
jumps (verified, lands in Ishenmar); run in the full survey it inherits Velaimar
from q-jump, which already has two belts, and stages in place.

## (b) w-traffic had no traffic — and it was not the distance

**The brief's hypothesis is wrong, and I can show it.** `ref.radius * dist` with
`radius = length * 1.5` puts the camera 247 m from a 30 m courier at `dist 5.5`.
That is about a fifth of the frame height — small, but nowhere near invisible.

The actual cause is a one-frame lag. `updateCamera` rebuilds the inspect rig
from `ref.absPos`, and it runs *before* `Fleet.update` moves the craft, so the
rig always aims one frame behind. Measured on the live page:

```
craft speed              14.533 km/s     →  0.24 km per frame at 60
inspect stand-off         0.2475 km
craft centre, projected   NDC (-2.799, -0.414)
```

The subject leaves the frame every single frame; NDC −2.8 is nearly three frame
widths off the side. That is the 74%-black rectangle.

Slowing the orbit rate is not sufficient either — `params.center` is the anchor
planet's *live* `absPos`, so the craft inherits the planet's own 0.4 km/s around
the star and still lands at NDC 0.22–0.32, by an amount that depends on the
capture machine's framerate. The shot now pins the craft to a cloned point
(`radius: 0` makes `PATHS.orbit` return the centre verbatim). Fleet leaves the
quaternion alone when the along-track sample does not move, so the hull keeps
the attitude it was flying at. q-jump rebuilds the fleet four shots later, so
nothing has to be restored.

`dist` swept with the hull's bounding box projected to NDC each time:

| dist | stand-off | hull width | hull height | centre NDC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5.5 | 256 m | 17% | 11% | (0.20, −0.02) |
| 3.2 | 153 m | 27% | 18% | (0.34, −0.03) |
| 2.6 | 117 m | 37% | 23% | (0.00, 0.00) |
| 2.2 |  99 m | 44% | 28% | (0.00, 0.00) |
| **1.8** | **81 m** | **54%** | **34%** | **(0.000, 0.000)** |
| 1.5 |  68 m | 64% | 41% | (0.00, 0.00) |

Took 1.8: reads as a vessel, still leaves the planet its third of the frame; 1.5
starts crowding the limb. Inspect's near plane is 8 m, so nothing here clips.

**Measured, before → after:** craft centre NDC (−2.799, −0.414) → (0.000, 0.000);
hull 0% of frame → 54% × 34%; black% 74.0 → 69.5 (most of a space frame is space,
which is why the black fraction is the wrong metric here and the hull box is the
right one).

**I did not touch `Fleet.js`.** `contacts()` reporting `radius = length * 1.5` is
not the defect, and that field feeds targeting, the scanner and the codex — a
shot-staging bug is not a reason to change what the game thinks a contact's
radius is.

## (c) z-landed-dusk — legs in the sky. Also y-landed had no ship at all.

**Root cause, common to both landings.** `land(b, {now:true})` skips the
*approach*, not the touchdown: it still seeds `landed.settle = SETTLE_TIME`
(4.2 s) and `updateSurface` eases the hull down `SETTLE_HEIGHT` over that. Every
landing frame in the set was therefore shot while the ship was still in the air.
Measured world Y of the hull in the ground scene, three seconds after `land`:
**192.8 m** on one capture and **157.9 m** on another — against a crane camera
that orbits 107–154 m out, 13–24 m up, and aims at 23 m. That is why y-landed is
a photograph of a meadow, and why z-landed-dusk has two gear feet dangling into
the top of frame with the hull above the edge. It is also why the frame was not
reproducible: where the hull was depended on how many frames the machine managed.

**Verdict on the brief's question** — frame the ship, don't hide it. A frame
named "landed at dusk" with no ship in it is the same failure class, and the
subtense works out (a 124 m hull at ~130 m against a 71° horizontal fov).

**Fix.**

1. `g.landed.settle = 0` in both landings. Hull parks at Y = 14.6 m, which is
   where the crane has been pointing all along.
2. The sky and the camera are one clock — `updateSurface` turns the sun by
   `landed.t * 0.012`, the crane takes its azimuth from `0.7 + landed.t * 0.030`
   — so the hour and the angle cannot be chosen independently. What *can* be
   chosen is the **day**: the sun is exactly periodic in `landed.t` with period
   2π/0.012 = 523.6 s, while the crane's azimuth advances 5π per day and its
   radius and height run on 89.8 s and 125.7 s besides. So the shot walks eight
   sunsets and measures each, at sun elevation 0.06 throughout:

   | day | hull w × h | ndc x span | unblocked | verdict |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 0 | 59% × 29% | −0.50 … 0.67 | 80% | ok, small |
   | 1 | 76% × 36% | −0.97 … 0.55 | 74% | nose on the left edge |
   | 2 | 83% × 43% | −0.75 … 0.90 | 79% | crowding the right edge |
   | 3 | 96% × 45% | −1.25 … 0.67 | 76% | clipped outright |
   | **4** | **69% × 35%** | **−0.61 … 0.77** | **84%** | **clean** |
   | 5 | 65% × 30% | −0.81 … 0.48 | 76% | ok |
   | 6 | 59% × 29% | −0.50 … 0.67 | 80% | ok |
   | 7 | 76% × 36% | −0.97 … 0.55 | 72% | nose on the left edge |

   Criterion: biggest hull that still clears ±0.85 of the frame. That picks day
   4, which a raycast against the ground scene also scores as least blocked by
   the near-field scatter — this planet's shrubs will happily park across the
   bow. Derived at capture time rather than written down, because the answer is
   a property of this landing site.
3. The clock is then **pinned** (`Object.defineProperty`, the way `framing.mjs`
   pins `freeLook`). Left running it drifts through the whole settle — 3.2 s is
   2.2° of sun and 5.5° of crane.
4. `g.fov = 44` while measuring. `updateCamera` eases the fov toward the crane's
   44 from the seated 68 at `dt*2`, so a search run straight after touchdown
   reads its early candidates through a wider lens than its late ones and picks
   a different day on a fast machine than on a slow one. **Two captures
   disagreed on the day until this line went in.**

**Measured, before → after:**

| | before | after |
|---|---|---|
| hull world Y at capture | 157.9 / 192.8 m (in flight) | 14.6 m (parked) |
| hull in z-landed-dusk | two gear feet at the top edge | 69% × 35%, fully inside ±0.85 |
| hull in y-landed | absent | parked, in frame |
| two independent captures of z-landed-dusk | 19.342% >10, mad 7.50 | **0.128% >10, mad 0.67** |
| z-landed-dusk mean / black% | 74.9 / 2.0 | 83.4 / 1.0 |
| y-landed mean / black% | 97.4 / 0.6 | 109.1 / 1.0 |

## Also added: `judgeset.mjs --only`

`node tools/judgeset.mjs --only z-landed --out /tmp/check`

All three of these defects survived because nobody re-shot the one frame they
had changed and looked at it — and a full rebuild is twenty minutes and
overwrites the directory a reviewer may be reading, which is a good way to talk
yourself out of checking. It filters the survey, the landings and the interfaces
alike, and shoots straight into `--out` (default `shots/check`).

It neither reads nor writes `shots/judge/`, and does not write `shots/` either.
That second part had to be fixed after I first wrote it: the `--only` branch was
running the survey at its default output, so a spot-check would drop a frame
into `shots/` taken under different conditions from the run that filled it —
standalone `t-belt` jumps system, so `shots/t-belt.png` would have come back
showing a *different star system* than the rest of that directory, which is the
exact poison this file's header comment is about. Verified after the fix:
`judgeset --only x-orbit --out <scratch>` leaves every mtime under `shots/`
unchanged and writes the frame only to the scratch directory.

## Performance

Nothing added to any frame's per-frame cost — every change is one-time staging
inside the capture harness. Full headed survey: t-belt 55 fps / 143 calls /
732k tris, w-traffic 54 fps / 133 calls / 453k tris, both above the run's median.
In the headless fps probe the belt view measures 60.0 fps while the *untouched*
x-orbit reference measures 32.2 in the same harness, which is the measure of how
much the capture path itself costs rather than the scene.

## Skipped

* **`Fleet.js`** — untouched, deliberately. See (b): the reported cause was not
  the cause, and `contacts().radius` feeds targeting and the codex.
* The **exposure drift** between two captures of the same landing frame (mean
  74.9 → 83.4 across revisions) is auto-exposure adaptation against settle time,
  is shared by all 24 frames, and is not a shot-staging bug. Out of scope here.
* `g-hot` still SKIPs — this system has no molten world. Pre-existing and
  correct behaviour.

## What the judge should check

1. `t-belt` is a belt, and is not `x-orbit`. Compare the two frames directly.
2. `w-traffic` has a courier at readable size against the planet limb, dead
   centre, and the same frame comes back on a re-run.
3. `z-landed-dusk` and `y-landed` both contain the parked ship, on its gear,
   with no geometry crossing a frame edge.
4. Shoot `z-landed-dusk` twice and imgdiff. It should be under a percent. If it
   is not, the clock pin or the fov pin has been lost.
5. `t-belt` now runs **last** in `SHOTS` and jumps system when it has to.
   Anything added after it will be shot in whatever system the belt search
   landed in.
