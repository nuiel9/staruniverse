# W3 — thin geometry, and what greeble.js can actually do about it

Files touched: `src/gfx/greeble.js` only.

---

## 1. The verdict on the brief's own item

**The previous agent was right about the dashes, and I can show why with pixels
rather than with an argument.** The candy-cane on v-station is lost *coverage*,
on geometry that `src/gfx/greeble.js` never sees the dimensions of. No material
term can put coverage back.

### The measurement that settles it

Take the shipped review frame, `shots/judge/v-station.png`, and dump the guy
wire that runs down through (912, 300). Integrating each row *across* the rod:

| y | 277 | 281 | 287 | 291 | 293 | 301 | 302 | 304 |
|---|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|-----|
| Σ luminance across the rod | 97 | 108 | 95 | 59 | 52 | 83 | **37** | 64 |

The rod is straight, unshadowed and lit by one key, so its shading cannot change
by a factor of three over twenty-five pixels. If this were sub-pixel *position*
drift under a working coverage filter, the integral would hold roughly constant
and only the distribution across the rod would shift. It does not hold. Energy
is being **destroyed**: a 0.5-px-wide sliver falls between the four MSAA sample
positions for some rows and lights none of them. That is a rasteriser result,
and it is upstream of every line of shading code in my file.

Three live experiments confirm it and rule out the shading explanations. All on
the `v-station` pose with the resolution controller pinned (it otherwise climbs
from native toward `prCeil` over several seconds and two runs are not the same
picture). Numbers are the whole-frame dash index defined in §4.

| experiment | dash index | reading |
|---|---|---|
| shipping, pinned pr 1.4 | **39.8 ± 0.7** (n=3) | the baseline |
| `window.__hullLight.uRimGain.value = 0` | worse | the backlit rim term is not the cause |
| `?msaa=0` | worse | MSAA is the *only* thing supplying continuity today |
| pinned pr 1.0 | 45.9 | tracks sample rate |
| pinned pr 4.0 | 47.3, with 2.6× more thin pixels resolved | tracks sample rate |

The defect scales with the sampling rate and with nothing else. Turning the one
additive shading term that could plausibly blow out a thin member (`RIM_HOOK`'s
`limb`) fully off makes it *worse*, not better, so I did not touch it.

### Why greeble.js specifically cannot fix it

1. **The offending members are authored in `src/world/Station.js`, not here.**
   `rail()` builds a 0.72 m bar for the top and knee rails and 0.60 m turned
   stanchions; `ladder()` builds 0.32 × 0.60 m stringers and 0.23 m rungs; the
   keel and flank cable runs are `CylinderGeometry(0.42 + k*0.12, …, 470, 8)`.
   At the review pose the frame is 0.58 m per pixel, so those project to
   **0.4–1.5 px**. greeble.js is handed the finished geometry.

2. **`GAUGE` never binds on a station.** The minimum-gauge law in this file is
   `1/300` of the structure, and every `truss()` call Station.js makes passes a
   radius far above it — `truss(90, 12, 4, 1.6)`, `truss(130, 30, 5, 3.0)`,
   `truss(HL-8, 11, 5, 0.55)`, `truss(len, 17, 3, 0.8)`. `gauge()` returns the
   caller's number unchanged in all four. Raising `GAUGE` would change freighter
   trusses and solar wings and would not touch one pixel of the defect.

3. **There is no per-vertex thinness channel on station geometry.** `aHull` is
   the one signal the kit has that knows a rail is a rail, and `bakeSurface()`
   is called only from `src/ship/hull.js`. Station geometry carries the default
   `aHull = (0, 0)`, so a vertex-shader "fatten anything thin" has nothing to
   key on there.

4. **A minimum *projected* width needs a uniform only the engine can supply.**
   Widening a silhouette by a fixed number of pixels requires the drawing-buffer
   height in the vertex shader, and three exposes no such uniform to a material.
   The renderer's buffer size also moves under the dynamic-resolution controller,
   so it cannot be baked. Reaching into `window.__game.renderer` from a material
   library to poll it is not something this file should do.

### What would have to change, and where

- `src/core/Engine.js` / `src/gfx/PostFX.js` — raise the MSAA sample count on
  the HDR target, or add TAA. A jittered projection with a history resolve is
  the only thing that genuinely *reconstructs* sub-pixel coverage; more samples
  only makes the lottery finer-grained. This is the real fix.
- `src/world/Station.js` — a gauge floor on `rail()`, `ladder()` and the cable
  runs. Cheaper, but it costs the scale story those parts exist to tell: the
  comment on `ladder()` says a rung is thirty centimetres *because everybody
  already knows that*, and a rung fat enough to survive this framing is 1.2 m.
- greeble.js *could* carry a minimum-projected-width displacement in
  `HULL_VERT_HOOK` — but only if `Engine.js` also fed `HULL_LIGHT` the drawing
  buffer height each frame. That is a two-file change and I was given one.

## 2. What I did instead, and it is in the same family

The brief's fallback names two things: large hull cylinders carrying a broad
even specular that reads plasticky, and panel-line density that is low for the
screen area those surfaces occupy. **Both have the same cause, and it is a
band-limiting hole in `seamLine()`.**

`plating()` authors a plate groove as `w = 0.026` — a fraction of a *plate*, not
a length. On a 1.8 m sheet that is a 47 mm gap, which is right. But at the
distance a station is judged from the plate is three pixels across, so the gap
is **a twelfth of a pixel**, and a twelfth of a pixel point-sampled is not a
panel line. It is a lottery: wherever a sample landed inside the gap the pixel
came back 58% dark, and everywhere else along the same joint it came back at
full plate value. That is why the largest cylinders in the frame are covered in
a stipple of isolated dark specks and have no lines on them for the key to break
against — and in motion that stipple crawls, which is the failure this file
already band-limits everything else against (`hullLod`, `lodT`, `lodB`, `lodP`,
the `nvar` → roughness widening). The albedo groove was the one term with no
band limit at all.

The fix is the same trade a mip chain makes. The groove is never allowed to fall
under about three quarters of a pixel wide, and its depth is scaled by exactly
the fraction of that pixel the real gap covers:

```
float we = max(w, px*0.18);
float aa = w / we;
```

The integral is unchanged — a 47 mm gap darkens a pixel by 47 mm worth of
darkness however far away it is — so nothing gets darker or lighter overall.
What changes is that the darkness lands on the joint *all along it* instead of
on one pixel in twelve. It converges correctly at both ends: once `px` falls
under about a seventh of `w` — any view closer than the hero shot — `we` is `w`,
`aa` is one and the surface is bit-for-bit what it was, and at the hero framing
itself `aa` is about 0.81, which measured as four hundredths of a per cent of
seam depth on u-shipclose; far enough out that a whole plate is under a pixel, the plating fades to
the uniform slightly-darker tone a pixel covering forty plates genuinely is,
rather than to noise.

`plating()` and `seamLine()` gained a `px` argument (one screen pixel in that
family's own plate units, derived from the `mpp` the caller already computes —
*not* from `fwidth()` inside the function, because `plating()` takes those
branches non-uniformly and a derivative there is undefined).

## 3. Before and after

The v-station pose is derived from the station root's own world attitude, so it
lands somewhere different on every boot and a two-run visual A/B compares
different geometry. Both states are therefore captured **from one boot** with
`g.update` stubbed out to freeze the world, toggled with a temporary uniform
that was removed before the file was left in the tree. Same pixels, same
exposure, same frame.

`0.00` is the shipping behaviour; `0.30` is what I shipped.

**Over one large hull cylinder** (the spoke at 630,180–770,340), which is the
surface the judge's note is about:

| groove floor | lottery specks /1k | seam run length | seam depth | seam pixels /1k |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **0.00 (before)** | **75.8** | **4.14 px** | 16.43% | 231.7 |
| **0.30 (shipped)** | **64.4  (−15%)** | **4.41 px  (+6.5%)** | 16.29% | 225.1 |
| 0.50 | 62.1 | 4.41 px | 16.14% | 223.0 |
| 1.00 | 58.8 | 4.55 px | 16.04% | 223.1 |

Whole frame, same session: lottery 60.9 → 56.0, run length 5.26 → 5.36, depth
16.19% → 16.00%.

0.50 and 1.00 score better on the counters and are **worse to look at**: at
those settings plating that was genuinely resolvable — the ring's plate grid —
visibly softens. The knee is where the lottery stops, not where the number stops
climbing. Crops in the scratch directory: `mag_S0_690_250.png` (dense stipple
over every spoke), `mag_S0p3_690_250.png` (stipple gone, ring still crisp),
`mag_S0p5_690_250.png` / `mag_S1_690_250.png` (ring softened).

**Hero hull, unchanged where it should be.** `u-shipclose` at 1:1 keeps every
panel line, plate boundary and hatch outline sharp
(`mag_HERO_800_450.png`); seam depth 17.75% against the shipped frame's 17.79%,
run length 7.83 against 7.74, lottery specks 10.1 against 14.9.

**Thin geometry, unchanged — as predicted.** Frozen A/B: dash index 37.83 →
36.76, breaks 5.9 → 5.3 per 1000 thin pixels. Marginal, and it is only the seam
stipple leaving the thin members' own surfaces. The dashes themselves are
untouched, because they are coverage.

**Cost.** One `max`, one divide and two multiplies per `seamLine` call; no new
texture fetch, no new noise octave, no new branch. `v-station` pinned at pixel
ratio 1.0 renders at **60.2 fps before, 60.3 fps after**. A frame-time A/B at
pixel ratio 3, where the hull's fragment shader is most of the frame, could not
resolve a difference: 25th-percentile frame time was 74.5 / 92.5 ms before and
78.3 / 90.7 / 90.4 ms after, overlapping, because other agents' GPU jobs on this
machine dominate the measurement.

## 4. The instruments, so the judge can re-run them

All in the scratch directory, none in the repo.

- `dash.mjs <png…>` — the candy-cane number. Finds pixels sitting on a structure
  three pixels wide or less (lit, with background two pixels away on both sides
  in x or in y) and walks *along* the member: `dashIdx` is the mean of
  `|L(next) − L(prev)| / (L + 8)`, ×100. `thinPx` is the coverage denominator,
  so a change that merely dims things is caught rather than rewarded. Frames are
  exposure-normalised first (70th percentile of lit pixels pinned to 40) —
  without that, how many frames of eye adaptation the machine managed inside the
  settle window moved the raw score by ten points.
- `lines.mjs [--rect x0,y0,x1,y1] <png…>` — panel-line density, coherence and
  the lottery count, measured only on the interior of the lit body so that
  silhouettes and the thin-member dashes cannot be counted as panel lines.
- `speck.mjs <tag> [--scene v|k|u] [--pr]` — captures with the resolution
  controller pinned; prints a loud warning if the page threw, because another agent
  editing this repo mid-run leaves the module graph broken for a few seconds and
  the frame that comes back is not this build's frame.
- `ab.mjs`, `cost.mjs` — the frozen-world paired A/B and the frame-time probe.

Reproducibility of the baseline, three independent boots at pinned pr 1.4:
dash index 40.54 / 39.07 / 39.92; seam run length 5.16 / 5.25 / 5.26; seam
density 230.4 / 239.0 / 238.2 per 1000. Anything moving these by more than about
2% is real.

## 5. What the judge should check

1. **That I did not overstate the fix.** The dashes on v-station are still
   there. Magnify (912, 300) on a fresh `v-station` frame and you will still see
   a broken chain. That item needs `Engine.js`/`PostFX.js` or `Station.js`.
2. **The large cylinders.** Compare `mag_S0_690_250.png` with
   `mag_S0p3_690_250.png` — same pixels, same frame, one uniform apart. The
   question is whether losing the dark stipple is worth what it takes with it.
   I judged yes, because that stipple is sub-pixel noise that crawls in motion
   and the per-plate tone that carries the panel read at distance is resolvable
   and untouched.
3. **The hero hull at 1:1**, `u-shipclose` and `k-ship`, for any softening of
   plating that was already sharp. I measured none, but that is the risk this
   change carries and it is the one worth a second pair of eyes.
4. **Anything I could not measure**: frame cost. The machine was too contended
   all session for a frame-time A/B to resolve four ALU ops. The 60 fps check at
   pixel ratio 1.0 is clean; the pixel-ratio-3 comparison is not.

## 6. What I skipped, and why

- **A sub-resolution specular convergence on thin members** (roughness → 1 where
  the geometric normal turns more than about half a radian per pixel). I built
  the case for it and then measured it away: inside the station's bounding box
  there are **zero** isolated bright specks with a peak at or above 120/255, and
  the guy wires peak at 42 against a frame whose 70th percentile is 70. The
  bright half of "white and black dashes" does not exist in this frame — the
  band limits already in the file are doing their job — so a specular band limit
  had nothing to fix and I did not ship a no-op.
- **Raising `GAUGE`.** It does not bind on any station geometry (§1.2), so it
  would change freighters and solar wings to fix nothing.
- **A minimum-gauge inflation inside `weld()` or `place()`.** `place()` sees each
  part's own bounding box and could floor its thin axes, which is the one honest
  in-file version of "minimum projected width". I did not ship it: the floor
  would have to be absolute metres with no camera to scale it, and a floor large
  enough to matter at this framing (~1.2 m) inflates ladder rungs past the point
  where they read as ladder rungs — which destroys the scale reference those
  parts exist to provide. It also fails silently on anything pre-rotated before
  `place()` (`strut()` does exactly that), so it would fix some members and not
  others. Worth revisiting only together with a real gauge pass in
  `Station.js`.
