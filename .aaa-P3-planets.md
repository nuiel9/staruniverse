# P3 — Planet shader band-limiting and night-side floor

**File touched: `src/world/planetShaders.js` — only.** No other file was needed;
the ring footprint is computed with `fwidth` in the fragment stage, so no new
uniform and no `Planet.js` edit. `node --check` passes; a brace/paren/bracket
balance check and a symbol-resolution check over all ten composed shader
strings pass (every function called resolves to a GLSL builtin or a definition
inside the composed source).

---

## 1. Rings — band-limiting (worklist item 1) — **done**

Verified the premise: `grep fwidth|dFdx|dFdy` over this file returned nothing
before this change.

`ringGaps`, `ringDensLo` and `ringDens` now each take `w`, the ring-radial
footprint of one pixel expressed in the same 0..1 `u` the frequencies are
written against — so `w*640` reads directly as "cycles of the ringlet octave
per pixel" and is comparable against Nyquist with no conversion. A new
`ringLim(cyclesPerPixel)` returns `1 - smoothstep(0.15, 0.60, x)` and each
octave set is faded toward **0.5** — its own mean, since every one of them is
`fbm*0.5 + 0.5`. Fading toward zero would make distant rings evaporate, which
is a different bug.

The 0.15..0.60 window starts under Nyquist because each term is an fbm carrying
2–4 octaves *above* the base frequency named in the call, so the top octave
crosses Nyquist while the base is at roughly an eighth of it. **These are the
numbers to retune, and only against a captured pan.**

`ringGaps` is now footprint-aware but the authored widths are **floors, never
replacements** — `max(authored, w*1.5)`. At `w = 0` the function reproduces the
old one bit for bit (I rewrote the two divisions as centre ± half-width so the
floor can widen them symmetrically: `smoothstep(0.030,0.055)` became
`smoothstep(0.0425-0.0125, 0.0425+0.0125)`, same numbers). The comment at
:203-212 arguing the A-ring outer rim is load-bearing for the cast shadow wins
and is respected — the edge only softens once it is narrower than a pixel, at
which point drawing it hard is drawing aliasing.

I added a **ceiling** the worklist did not ask for: `min(w, 0.16)*1.5`. Edge-on,
one pixel covers the whole ring, the footprint runs away, and an unbounded ramp
reaches from the inner edge past the outer one and multiplies the entire
annulus to zero — a ring seen along its plane would have *vanished* instead of
collapsing to a bright line. That is a regression the naive form ships with.

**`ringShadow` does not use `fwidth`, deliberately.** `CLOUD_FRAG` reaches it
past two data-dependent early returns (`geoNoLEarly < -0.19` and `acc <= 0.004`)
and a derivative in non-uniform control flow reads lanes that stopped
executing — the failure this project already documents at `Surface.js:3506`. So
`ringShadow` takes the *surface point's* footprint `pd`, which each caller
measures in straight-line code at the top of its own `main`, and carries it
through the chain rule: `hp = d + lObj*t`, `t = -d.y/lObj.y`, giving
`pd*(1 + 1/|lObj.y|)` over the ring's radial extent. That second term is the
projection *tightening*: a star near the ring plane squeezes the whole shadow
into a narrow equatorial band (Saturn at equinox throws a line, at solstice
broad bands), so that is exactly when the structure is packed into fewest
pixels and needs most filtering. Three call sites updated
(`SURFACE_FRAG`, `GAS_FRAG`, `CLOUD_FRAG`).

**Cost:** ~3 extra `smoothstep`s per `ringDens` and ~6 per `ringShadow`, on
fragments that already run 12 and 21 simplex evaluations respectively. Under
1%. No extra texture fetches.

## 2. Terrain normals (worklist item 2) — **done**

`float e = uTexel*1.6` became `clamp(length(fwidth(dt)), uTexel*1.6,
uTexel*24.0)` — the old constant survives unchanged as the floor, so nothing
changes at the range the bake was sized for, and the lag grows once a pixel is
wider than 1.6 texels. Capped at 15× the floor because `fwidth(dt)` diverges at
the limb, where an uncapped lag would flatten the one band of the disc whose
relief reads as silhouette.

Forward difference → **central difference**, gated on `uSharp` and blended by
it. The forward form estimates the slope 0.8 of a texel away from the pixel it
shades; across a bilinearly magnified height field that bias lands on the
interpolation grid and returns faceted steps. `relTex` is recomputed as
`max(|hT-hTm|,|hB-hBm|)*0.5` so it still means "height change per lag `e`" and
every consumer (coastline `coastW`, the shoreline wiggle amplitude, the
night-lights `valley` denominator) keeps its meaning. The night-lights valley
term was hoisted to `valleyH` and, close up, becomes a real 4-tap Laplacian
rather than a one-sided guess.

The `uDetail` block's fixed lag `T*0.35` on a frequency-260 fBm became
`max(0.35, px*260.0)` — at the range the `uDetail` ramp switches on (~0.0011
rad/px) that is a no-op by construction — **with the difference renormalised by
`0.35/dl`**, because what is added is a difference and not a derivative and a
wider lag would otherwise silently deepen the bumps. A footprint fade
(`1 - smoothstep(0.0014, 0.0040, px)`) rides both the slope term and the albedo
mottling; thresholds sit above the footprint at the ramp's own onset so it is
inert at the tuned range and only rescues wide-FOV / small-canvas cases the
angular-size ramp cannot see.

**Cost — this is the only real cost in the package: +2 `textureCube` fetches
per pixel, gated to `uSharp > 0.004` (angular size ≳ 0.1 rad).** The surface
pass goes from 3 to 5 cube taps in the close case (plus the existing terrain-
and cloud-shadow taps). At full frame that is roughly a 5–12% increase in that
pass's texture load; at any distance where a planet covers few pixels it is
noise. It is deliberately paid where the artefact is, not everywhere.

## 3. Night side (worklist item 3) — **structural half done, tuning half skipped**

Did both mechanism fixes, which are magnitude-independent:
- `textureCube(uEnv, N)` → `textureCube(uEnv, Ng)`. The term stands in for the
  sky over this point; on a sphere that is the geometric hemisphere. Fetching
  a PMREM of the nebula with the *bumped* normal made every ridge sample a
  different quarter of the sky, which is the blotchy high-contrast patches with
  black bites. Same fetch, different argument — free.
- `env*0.22` → `env*0.22*shadowMask`, so the sky fill dies at the terminator.
  `uAmbient` keeps the `0.16` floor; the sky does not get one.

**Did not touch the `0.16` floor or the `0.22` weight.** Those are magnitudes
and P2 is moving `uEnv`'s contents (the nebula bake, plane raised ~3×) out from
under both of them right now. `planetShaders.js:622-623` argues the night side
must fall to near-nothing and it is right; this change serves that.

> **Re-judge note:** the night side changed *structurally* in the same pass its
> input changed *in magnitude*. Judge the two together on a re-shot c-terran and
> b-terran, and tune the floor only after.

## 4. Gas bands (worklist item 4) — **chroma done; the warp half did not survive contact with the code**

**Skipped, with reason:** "scale the domain-warp amplitude by
`|d(band)/d(latitude)|` so warping churns only at boundaries" is already
implemented. `GAS_FRAG` computes `edge = 1.0 - abs(sin(phase0))` — peaking
exactly where the zonal wind reverses — and gates the two fine warp fields with
`(w2*0.70 + w3*0.30)*(0.35 + edge)`. The finding is a duplicate of shipped
code. I did not lower the `0.35` floor; the surrounding comments defend floors
of this kind explicitly and at length.

**Done, but not the way the worklist words it.** The real gap is that the block
labelled "hue as well as value" has nowhere to go: every mix lands between
`uC0..uC3`, and in all six gas palettes those are four *values of one hue*
(`0x150c06`→`0xf8ecd0` is one brown; `0x061420`→`0xe4f4fa` is one blue). That
was a correct choice — the note over those palettes records that value
separation was the first thing the disc needed — but it means no mix between
them can produce chroma. The missing axis has to be synthesised in the shader.
The palettes live in `generate.js`, which is not my file; I did not go there.

So: a **saturation-and-warmth shift about each band's own luma**, keyed to a
2-octave field of `latW` at frequency 2.6 — a function of latitude alone, hence
continuous everywhere and near-constant along a belt, which `hueF` cannot be
(it rides `w1`/`w2`, sampled in a frame squashed 7.5× in latitude, so it drifts
along a belt's length). Belts swing ±50% of chroma, zones ±22%, because a zone
is the ammonia showing through and has less chromophore to vary. This is also
the right physics: Jupiter's belts are one chromophore at different
concentrations, not different pigments.

**Deviation from the worklist, flagged deliberately:** it says "keyed on
quantised latitude". I did *not* key it to `hash11(bid)` with
`bid = floor(phaseA/PI)`. `phaseA` carries up to 1.55 rad of warp against a
band period of π, so `bid` is non-monotonic in latitude and a hash-keyed colour
would draw hard-walled islands of tint — the exact Voronoi-wall failure the
night-lights comment in this same file spends a paragraph refusing to
re-commit. A smooth low-frequency field of `latW` gives per-belt identity with
no walls.

Applied to `belt` and `zone` *before* `mix(belt, zone, band)`, on purpose, so
every deliberate feature colour downstream — ovals, the great spot's
`vec3(1.70,0.62,0.36)`, the roll crests, the festoons — is left exactly alone.
A late application would have desaturated the Great Red Spot by latitude.

**Cost:** +2 simplex evaluations and ~10 ALU per pixel on gas giants, against
the 60+ simplex evaluations `GAS_FRAG` already runs. Under 2% of that pass.

## What the re-judge should look at specifically

1. **Rings in motion, not in a still.** q-jump, i-rings, d-gas. Moire is a
   motion defect; a still cannot show what items 1's constants are for. If a
   ring now reads *too* smooth at mid distance, the fade window
   (`ringLim`, 0.15..0.60) is the single knob — raise both ends together.
2. **Mid-distance moons reading flatter than before.** This is the one place
   item 2 can over-correct: `length(fwidth())` sums both screen derivatives
   rather than taking the larger, so the footprint estimate is conservative by
   up to ~2× and the normal lag grows a little sooner than strictly necessary.
   That is the correct direction to err for motion stability, but check h-dry
   and f-ice at mid range against the pre-change set. The floor
   (`uTexel*1.6`) guarantees nothing changed at the close range the bake was
   tuned for.
3. **The ring shadow on a giant's cloud tops.** `ringDensLo` is now filtered
   too, and at close range the 190-cycle `fine` term is genuinely past Nyquist
   in the shadow's projection and drops out entirely. The 34-cycle band
   structure and all the gap structure survive. Confirm the shadow still reads
   as the edge of a solid object crossing the tops — `planetShaders.js:203-212`
   says that hard line is the whole tell, and it is the thing most at risk from
   this item.
4. **Night side, as a pair with P2** — see the note under item 3.
5. **Frame time on q-jump and h-dry**, per the worklist. The +2 cube taps are
   the only non-trivial cost in this package and they land on exactly the
   frames that were already the most expensive.
