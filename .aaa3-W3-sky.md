# W3 — sky: occlusion and streaks

**Files I own:** `src/gfx/Sky.js`, `src/gfx/PostFX.js`.
**Files I changed: none.** Both briefed defects were measured out of those two
files. What follows is the evidence, the exact fix for the file that does own
(a), and the numbers the judge should re-check.

Everything below was measured on the running game at 1600x900 through
Playwright against the dev server, not reasoned about. Probes live in
`/private/tmp/claude-501/-Users-krainats-staruniverse/da01630a-d293-4234-9d4f-6798f616e952/scratchpad/`
(`w3fleet.mjs`, `w3final.mjs`, `w3iso.mjs`, `w3point.mjs`, `w3streak.mjs`,
`drow.mjs`, `diff.mjs`, `clus.mjs`, `row.mjs`).

Two things had to be got right before any number meant anything:

* **Freeze the simulation.** `main.js` calls `game.update(dt)` every frame, so
  replacing it with a no-op stops the ship, the orbits and the spin while the
  renderer keeps drawing. My first three runs were taken without it and the
  numbers wandered by 150 levels between two grabs 1.2 s apart — the f-ice pose
  carries `throttle:0.14`, so the camera is *moving* in the judge's own frame.
* **Do not trust a raw A-minus-B.** Hiding fourteen thousand additive sprites
  moves the eye adaptation, which regrades the whole frame; the first
  "star-over-planet" measurement came back with 88,000 changed pixels, none of
  them stars. Note for anyone reusing the trick: pinning `engine.dt = 0` does
  **not** freeze the adaptation, because `Engine.render` passes
  `dt: this.dt || 0.016` and `0` is falsy. Where it mattered I either fitted the
  regrade out (median stars-on level per stars-off bucket, a monotone curve
  identical everywhere in frame) or kept the toggle small enough that the
  regrade stayed inside a couple of levels.

## (a) Stars drawing over opaque bodies — it is not the star field

The two sprites the judge dumped at (1169,453) and (1140,483) on f-ice are
still there. They are **not stars**. Measured, in order:

| test (f-ice, frozen) | peak in a 15x15 box at (1174,453) / (1156,501) |
|---|---|
| base | 254 / 254 |
| star field hidden (`setLayer('stars',false)`) | **254 / 254** |
| nebula background hidden | 254 / 254 |
| every planet group hidden, one at a time (16 bodies) | 254 / 254 |
| dust, bloom, flare, streak, sun hidden | 254 / 254 |
| **fleet hidden** | **147 / 109** (i.e. the moon's own lit surface) |

The sprites are the fleet's navigation beacons. `makeBeacons()` in
`src/world/Fleet.js` (~line 174) builds them with

```js
transparent: true, depthWrite: false, depthTest: false,
blending: THREE.AdditiveBlending,          // uMinPx: 5.0, renderOrder 20
```

`depthTest:false` is why a craft **beyond** the ochre moon paints its lights on
the moon's disc, and `uMinPx:5.0` is why each one reads as a flat five-pixel
tile — which is exactly why they were mistaken for the old star sprite.

**The fix, verified at runtime without editing the file.** Flipping
`depthTest = true` on the ten beacon materials (`w3fleet.mjs`):

* peaks at the two spots: **254 → 143 and 254 → 94** — the moon's own lit
  surface showing through where the sprite used to be. The fleet-hidden control
  read 147/109 at the same spots, but on a different boot, so treat those two
  pairs as the same order of magnitude rather than as a match; the whole-frame
  diff below is the claim that carries weight;
* whole-frame diff: **1691 pixels change, in exactly two clusters** — 776 px at
  (1141..1198, 471..511) and 822 px at (1159..1191, 438..470), the two sprites
  and their bloom. **No beacon anywhere else in the frame is lost**; 75 pixels
  move the other way, all of them by 3-4 levels.
* crop `scratchpad/chk_dt.png` beside `scratchpad/w3_moon.png`: the disc is
  clean, the beacons outside the disc are untouched.

I did not apply it — Fleet.js is not mine and another agent may be in it. One
risk worth checking when someone does: a beacon quad is centred on a light's
position **on the hull**, so with depth testing on, a lamp sitting flush with
plating could be swallowed by its own craft at close range. Check `u-shipclose`
and `w-traffic` after the change; if lamps vanish, push the beacon centre a
fraction of the craft radius toward the eye in `BEACON_VERT` rather than
reverting the depth test.

**And the star field itself is clean — this is the part that was mine to
verify.** With the fleet hidden and the sim frozen, star field on vs off, inside
each body's *screen disc* (centre projected, angular radius `f·tan(asin(r/d))`,
sampled to 0.90 of it so the limb and its transparent atmosphere — where a star
behind a shell legitimately shows through — are excluded):

| disc | disc pixels | px gaining ≥3 | px gaining ≥8 | largest connected cluster ≥8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| desert moon (1146,460) r=45 | 5,188 | 5 | **0** | none |
| ice planet (654,550) r=445 | 490,780 | 766 | 64 | **2 px**, max +18 |

Sixty-four pixels over half a million, in **61 separate clusters of one or two
pixels**, is not a sprite: those are single-pixel FXAA flips on crater-rim edges
where the star field's bloom veil changes the local contrast by a level or two.
For comparison a fleet beacon on the same disc is a 15-20 px blob at +150. The
`depthTest:false` + `transparent:false` + `renderOrder:-1000` scheme in Sky.js
does hold; nothing there needed changing and I changed nothing.

## (b) Beaded streaks — the anamorphic pass is not what is beading

**The anamorphic streak is continuous.** Measured on the strongest source in the
game (j-star, `bodyRef:g.bodies[0], dist:16`), isolating the term as
base-minus-`enabled.streak=false` (that toggle moved 4 pixels the other way, so
the regrade is negligible here) and dumping the row through the bar's centre:

* extent **667 px** (x=433..1099 at y=482), peak +38 levels, monotone rise and
  fall;
* every local minimum inside the bar's body sits at **0.95-1.00** of the mean of
  its bracketing maxima. The only ratios under 0.9 are at the tails where the
  term is 2-3 levels — 8-bit quantisation, one level of noise on a value of two;
* no sub-structure at 4 px (quarter-res texel), 16 px (pass 2, `uStep 4`) or
  52 px (pass 3, `uStep 13`) — the three periods a beaded chain would have to
  have if it came from these passes.

Repeated with the pass pushed far past its shipped strength
(`streakThreshold 2.1 → 0.25`, `uStreak 0.013 → 0.35`, `w3point.mjs`) so the term
is 70 levels instead of 3: same answer — 545 px of bar at y=555, dips 0.93-1.00
through the body.

**What the judge is looking at is diagonal, and a pass that only offsets along
x cannot draw a diagonal.** Both streak passes sample `vUv + vec2(fi*uStep*uTexel.x, 0.0)`.
The chains in the judge's frames run at roughly 35 degrees. Identified by
toggling, frozen, on the station shot: hiding the dust removes them — 4,090
pixels drop, the top ones a diagonal run at (519,784) (527,780) (532,777)
(541,772) (550,767) (559,762) (564,759) (572,755) (577,752) (582,749) (595,742)
(604,737), peaks up to **189 levels**. They are `src/world/Dust.js` motes
streaked along velocity. At 1:1 they read as thin continuous lines
(`scratchpad/fin_dust.png`); magnified they are a line of lit pixels, which is
what "resolves into separated dots" is describing. The dim orange chain in the
judge's `x-orbit` at (928,142)..(962,165) is the same shape and the same
brightness class (10-40 levels) — a world-space chain of point sources, not a
lens effect.

**Worth knowing for whoever revisits this:** a star cannot produce an anamorphic
streak in this build at all. The streak is seeded from `blurMips[1]`, which is
quarter resolution, so a three-pixel star is averaged over sixteen pixels before
the 2.1-stop threshold ever sees it. Dropping the threshold to 0.25 and raising
the weight 27x still produced **zero** streak on the brightest star in x-orbit
(row through it: flat, 0 to -1 levels) while lighting a 545 px bar off the
planet's sunlit limb. That is deliberate — the comment above `STREAK_FRAG` says
what happened the last time stars were allowed into the streak buffer (dozens of
mid-magnitude stars in a regular grid, each wearing a blue dash) — and I left it
alone.

## What the judge should check

1. **f-ice will still show the two sprites on the ochre moon until Fleet.js
   changes.** Do not read that as the star field: the measurement above shows
   the field contributes 0 pixels inside the moon's disc and 2-pixel edge flips
   inside the ice planet's. The one-line fix and its before/after are in (a).
2. Re-check occlusion the same way I did rather than by eye: freeze
   `game.update`, hide the fleet, toggle `setLayer('stars', …)`, and count
   *clusters* inside a projected disc. A single-pixel difference is not a sprite.
3. If a streak still reads as beaded somewhere, check the direction first. If it
   is not horizontal it is not this pass, and the next suspects are Dust.js and
   Fleet.js's beacons, in that order.
