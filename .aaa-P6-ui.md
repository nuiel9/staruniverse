## What changed

### 1. Type floor — 11px, tracking cut ~⅓

A `--t-min: 11px` token on `:root` with the argument for the number written
beside it. Every player-facing size below it went up and had its tracking cut
at the same time, so a raised label occupies roughly the width it did before
and the market table did not get taller by a third. The offenders the worklist
named — `.dk-tag` 8px, `.dk-table th` / `.dk-note` / `.dk-act button` /
`.cm-line.sys` at 9-10px — plus the whole 9px secondary layer running through
the archive, the star-map side panel, the chart readout, the surface chart, the
boot screen and the language chips.

Two deliberate exceptions, argued in the file rather than overlooked:

* **The canopy layer** (`.mk-lbl` 8.5→10.5, its `em` 7.5→9.5, `.lg` 9.5→10.5)
  sits one step under the panel floor. A marker label is glanced at, there can
  be a dozen at once, and every pixel it gains comes off the thing it points
  at. 8.5 was too small to glance at; 10.5 with the tracking cut occupies
  almost exactly the old width.
* **`.dk-tag` and `.dk-rep i` at 10px.** The tag is set in capitals only, so
  its cap height at 10px is the same ink as a mixed-case string at ~13.5px —
  bigger than the commodity name beside it. At 11 the badge became the loudest
  word on the row.

`#perf` (9px) was left alone: it is `display:none` unless you ask for it and is
not player-facing.

The `@media (max-height:520px)` block — which is exactly what a **1024×480**
capture trips — set `.lg` to 7.5px and `.mk-lbl` to 7.5px, undoing the floor on
the one viewport JUDGE.md explicitly asks to be judged at. Both raised to 10px,
and the vertical saving now comes from padding and line-height instead. Same
for the `max-width:760px` block's `.lg{font-size:8px}`.

The `html[lang="th"]` size overrides became *downgrades* once the floor moved:
`.dk-table th` at 10px is now 12px.

### 2. Comms — the 700px hole, and the measure

`.comms-feed{flex:1}` made the feed eat the entire column, pinning the
transcript to the top of a 900px screen and the verbs to the bottom.
`margin-top:auto` with no grow gives the feed its content height and pushes it
onto the verb row, so the exchange and the reply to it are always within a
couple of lines of each other and the empty space collects *above*, where it
reads as air rather than as a gap inside a conversation. `min-height:0` +
`overflow-y:auto` are retained so long transcripts still scroll, and
`_line()`'s `scrollTop` still follows the bottom. (`margin-top:auto`, not
`justify-content:flex-end`, which makes overflow unreachable in a scrolling
flex container.)

**Deviation from the worklist:** it asked for the measure to move from
`.cm-line` to `.comms-feed`. It went onto `.comms-body` instead — `max-width:552px`
with a 24px gutter, ~504px of text, about 62 characters of this monospace at
13.5px, the same measure the archive's prose already uses. On the feed alone
the transcript would have had one rag and the deal strip and verb row another;
on the body all three share one column, which is what the "ragged measure"
finding is actually about. The old `56ch` on `.cm-line` was a per-line value in
`ch`, so a 10px system note wrapped at ~350px and a 13.5px reply at ~790px.

Short-viewport rules for `.comms-*` added — the worklist correctly notes it was
the one panel with no responsive block at any breakpoint. Also a
`max-width:760px` block for it.

### 3. Comms — the screen answers itself

* **Affordability moved in front of the click.** `_fillable()` and `_blocker()`
  compute how much of the lot can actually move *at the price on the button*,
  before the control is drawn. ACCEPT carries a second line saying what
  pressing it does (`−100 cr`, or `2/5 · −40 cr` when the fill is partial), and
  is `disabled` with the reason inline (`hold is full` / `not enough credits` /
  `you hold none`) when it cannot go through at all. `Comms.js:227` used to
  silently part-fill, or refuse *after* the click with the reason in a system
  line. The guard inside `_act('accept')` stays — `_act` is also driven
  directly by `tools/judgeset.mjs` and refusing silently would be worse — but
  it is no longer how a player finds out.
* **The deal strip.** The quote only ever existed inside the contact's prose
  ("20 a unit and we both walk away smiling"), so the most important number on
  the screen was the least prominent and there was nothing to judge it against.
  A `.cm-deal` block, written as the last element of the transcript and
  *replaced* rather than stacked on each counter, carries the unit price at
  26px, the lot total, and the deviation from the commodity's standard value —
  which is the number that actually decides the press. Green/amber is derived
  from `o.mode`, not from the sign: under standard is the player's win when
  they are selling, over standard when they are buying.
* **Two-sided transcript.** `_said()` echoes the chosen verb on the right in
  cyan. It echoes the button's already-translated string, so it cost no new
  prose and no new Thai; and it is raised inside `_act` rather than read off
  the clicked element on purpose, because `_act` is driven headlessly by the
  judge harness — an echo that needed a mouse would be missing from exactly the
  frames that get judged.
* **Triad differentiated.** ACCEPT is amber with the `--slate` cut corner,
  COUNTER keeps the cyan outline, WALK AWAY is a borderless ghost. Three
  identical cyan boxes offered no opinion about which one the conversation was
  waiting for.
* **Four English string literals** in `Comms.js` (both settlement lines, the
  paid-rumor line, both refusal lines) were the last untranslated player-facing
  text in the negotiation, and they are the ones a player reads most since
  every completed trade ends in one. Keyed and translated.

### 4. Market

* **`VS BASE` column.** `price / c.base - 1` as a signed percentage. "43 cr" is
  not information to anyone who has not memorised the eight base values;
  "+79%" is the whole trade decision. Green cheap / amber dear, matching what
  PRODUCES and WANTED already mean on the same row so the two signals reinforce
  rather than introducing a second colour law. Within ±5% it greys out. Hidden
  below 640px — it is the only column that is derivable, so it goes first.
* **Row tints 3% → 9%, plus a saturated 2px left edge.** 3% alpha is about one
  level out of 255 on this ground: the tint was in the stylesheet and not in
  the frame, and the whole "buy the green rows here, sell into their green rows"
  pitch rests on seeing it across a table. Every first cell reserves the 2px
  whether or not it is coloured, so a tinted row does not sit two pixels right
  of an untinted one; the same on `th:first-child` so the head still aligns.
* **Disabled verbs are ghosts.** `border-color:transparent` rather than a dim
  colour, so the box stays exactly the same size and nothing reflows when a
  purchase enables it. Every row used to show two full controls with only one
  live.
* **Scroll affordance.** `scrollbar-color` opts Chromium out of overlay
  scrollbars so the track exists in a still frame, plus a 70px fade on
  `.dock-body`. A guillotined table with a flat bottom edge and no visible
  scrollbar is indistinguishable from one where the content simply ends — the
  review set caught it mid-word in the crew list.

### 5. Chart readout and the rest

* **`.nv-banner` is no longer the quietest element** in the panel. It is the
  readout's verdict — the line that says whether you can go — and it was a grey
  rule on a 10% grey wash under a column of bright numbers. Full border, the
  cut corner, more ground, 12px.
* **`.nv-banner.stop`** added — see skipped work below.
* **`--slate` / `--cut` moved from `#hud` to `:root`.** Every panel is a
  *sibling* of `#hud`, so `var(--slate)` resolved to nothing there, and a
  clip-path that fails looks identical to never having asked for one. The shape
  language was defined once and applied to one prompt chip. It is now on the
  primary ACCEPT, DEPART, SELL CHARTS, the fold banner and the deal strip.
* **`.hud-lang` de-emphasised.** A solid cyan chip in the top-left corner made
  the least consequential switch in the game the brightest object in the frame,
  brighter than the reticle. On the boot screen the fill is right — choosing a
  language is the only thing that screen asks. In the cockpit it keeps the
  shape, loses the fill, and hover brings it back.
* **`#narrStack` suppressed under a panel.** Done as
  `body:has(.panel:not(.hidden)) #narrStack{opacity:0}` rather than the
  `body.panel-open` class the worklist proposed: `:has()` needs no JS handshake
  and — the reason it wins here — it covers panels toggled from files this
  package does not own. It also stays suppressed through the 220ms `.closing`
  fade, since a closing panel has lost neither class. Position was *not*
  touched; `style.css:141-153` records two prior repositions each landing in a
  new obstacle, and that comment wins.

## What it costs

**No frame-time cost.** Every change is DOM/CSS on layers that only exist while
a panel is open. No new `backdrop-filter`, no new compositing layer that
animates, no shader touched. Specifics:

* `body:has(...)` is one rule with `body` as the subject; Chromium re-checks it
  when an element gains or loses `.panel`/`.hidden`, i.e. once per panel open.
* `.dock-body::after` is a static linear-gradient painted once per panel open.
* `scrollbar-color` forces classic (non-overlay) scrollbars — no cost, and it
  is what makes the affordance visible in a still.
* `DockScreen.render()` runs at 1 Hz and gained one `<td>` per row over eight
  rows.
* `Comms._deal()` does one `innerHTML` write per player action.

Net: `#narrStack` stops being composited while a panel is open, which is a
(trivial) win.

## What I deliberately did not do

1. **The numeric fold cost on the chart banner.** `HoloMap.js:754` writes
   `t('map.beyondDrive')` in place of a percentage when `cost > 1`, and
   `:764-769` picks the banner text and toggles `.go`. That file belongs to P7.
   I added the `.nv-banner.stop` CSS and left a comment naming the one-line
   change that uses it — `.toggle('stop', !canJump && !isCur)` beside the
   existing `.toggle('go', canJump)`, which turns the three blocked states red
   and leaves "you are here" neutral. **Half of that finding is still open and
   it is P7's half.** I considered and rejected deriving the state in CSS via
   `#navinfo:has(.nv-rows span.rd) .nv-banner:not(.go)`: it misses the
   no-charge case and would paint "YOU ARE HERE" red on a dry tank.
2. **Re-capturing at dpr 1, 2:1 and 1024×480.** `tools/judgeset.mjs` shoots the
   three `ui-*` frames at `--dpr 2`, `1600×900` only. `tools/*` is P10's. See
   the re-judge notes below.
3. **Repositioning `#narrStack`.** The comment at `style.css:141-153` argues
   position is the wrong lever and it is right; suppression is the lever.

Nothing in the worklist's P6 list turned out to be wrong on contact with the
source. Every claim in it that I checked held: `.dk-tag` was 8px,
`.comms-feed{flex:1}` was the 700px hole, `max-width:56ch` was on `.cm-line`,
`Comms.js` did silently part-fill, the tints were at 3%, and `--slate` was
genuinely unused everywhere except `#prompt`.

## What the re-judge should look at specifically

* **Shoot `ui-a`, `ui-b`, `ui-c` at `--dpr 1` as well as 2.** A legibility
  verdict taken only at dpr 2 cannot see a type-floor defect at all — 8px is 16
  device pixels there, exactly on the line, and 8 device pixels on a 1× panel.
  This is the single most important addition to the capture set for this package.
* **Shoot at a second aspect ratio, per JUDGE.md.** 2:1 (1600×800) and
  **1024×480**. The 1024×480 case is the one that trips
  `@media (max-height:520px)`, which is where the old sub-floor sizes lived and
  where `.comms-*` had no rules at all until now.
* **`ui-c-comms` specifically:** the transcript should now be a two-sided
  exchange sitting immediately above the verb row, with a deal strip carrying
  `20 · cr/unit · −17% vs standard · 2 × 20 = −40 cr` and an amber ACCEPT with
  its own second line. Check the ACCEPT sub-line against the actual hold and
  ledger the harness leaves the ship in — if the frame catches it *disabled*
  with a reason, that is the fix working, not a bug.
* **`ui-a-market`:** the `VS BASE` column should read `+8% / +79% / −59%` etc.
  against the base values in `Economy.js:24-31`. Confirm the PRODUCES/WANTED
  rows are now visibly tinted and that the head row's first column still lines
  up with the body rows (both carry the 2px reservation). Confirm the bottom
  fade and the scrollbar track are both present — if the scrollbar is missing,
  the capture is running with overlay scrollbars forced and the fade is doing
  the whole job alone.
* **`ui-b-chart`:** the EN/TH chip top-left should no longer be the brightest
  object in the frame, and the `NEBULA TOO DENSE` banner should now have a
  border and a cut corner. It will still be grey, not red — that is item 1
  above, waiting on P7.
* **Thai.** Run any frame with `html[lang="th"]`. Every new key carries both
  slots and the tracking-suppression list was extended to the new classes, but
  the Thai in `comms.aboard` / `comms.away` / the three blocker fragments is
  mine and has not been read by a native speaker.
